What is LingPALS?

A welcoming, peer-assisted place to explore language together.

LingPALS brings students together to explore Linguistics and English Language side by side. It is a low-pressure space to ask questions, compare approaches and practise ideas — a complement to your lectures, tutorials and independent study.

What LingPALS offers

  • Explore language alongside other students.
  • Compare approaches to lecture content.
  • Practise examples aloud.
  • Work through structures together.
  • Share study strategies.

The character of the table

Peer-led, active and collaborative. You do not need perfect preparation or all the answers — just curiosity and a question to compare. Start where you are.

Alongside your teaching

LingPALS works with your degree, never instead of it. Online notes help you get started; the sessions are where ideas become conversation.

Why might I join?

Because language makes more sense out loud, with others.

Bring a question

Take questions into a low-pressure peer space and try ideas without judgement.

Hear other approaches

See how other students tackle the same topic — and compare it with yours.

Build confidence

Grow by explaining, listening and trying ideas together. Try it together.

Find community

Belong to a friendly community of language-curious students.

What happens in a peer-assisted learning session?

Small groups, shared examples, plenty of comparing notes.

Arrive & settle

Join the table with whatever you are working on. No preparation required.

Bring a question

Share a topic, an example or a tree you are unsure about.

Compare notes

Work through structures and sounds together, explaining as you go.

Practise aloud

Try pronunciations, build phrases and test ideas with peers.

Take it forward

Leave with new approaches and a note or two to explore next.

How can I explore useful Linguistics resources?

Pick up a note from the table.

The paper notes around the leaflet are curated pathways into helpful Linguistics resources — phonetics, syntax, morphology, pronunciation and grammar. Explore a topic online, then take a question into the room, discuss it with peers, and build confidence through participation.

Every note is a real starting point:

Explore a topic online → take a question into the room → discuss it with peers → build confidence together.
